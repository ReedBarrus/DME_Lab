# Consequence-Directed Realization Selection

**Status:** PROJECTION
**Scientific standing:** NONE
**Architecture authority:** NONE
**Implementation authority:** NONE
**Routing authority:** NONE

This note records a candidate bridge between DME conservation-through-transformation, realized worker identity, local-model qualification, and future consequence-guided routing.

Repository evidence outranks this projection.

This projection does **not** establish a scheduler, router, qualification framework, persistent operator grammar, autonomous worker ecology, or model hierarchy.

---

## 1. Candidate Principle

A realization is not merely the identity of the thing performing an action.

It may be treated as a candidate **transformation instrument** whose suitability is relative to the transformation being requested and to the distinctions that transformation is supposed to conserve, vary, expose, or generate.

Candidate relation:

```text
source state / observation basis
        ↓
declared desired consequence
        ↓
requested transformation
        ↓
required invariance / allowed variation
        ↓
consequence boundary and resource budget
        ↓
eligible realization candidate
        ↓
action / observation
        ↓
comparison against declared conservation requirement
```

The important claim is not that a realization is globally good or bad.

The projected claim is narrower:

> realization suitability is conditional on the transformation regime and consequence boundary under which it is asked to act.

---

## 2. Why This Matters

Current local-automation work already retains realized execution basis rather than treating a model name as a sufficient identity.

Current conservation work already asks what changed and what remained invariant through transformation.

The missing bridge is to make the requested conservation or divergence regime relevant to **which realization is eligible to perform the transformation at all**.

Without this bridge, routing can collapse into an undifferentiated question such as:

```text
Which model is best?
```

The projected replacement is closer to:

```text
What transformation is being requested?
What distinctions must survive?
What distinctions may vary?
What distinctions are desired to vary?
What consequence ceiling applies?
What resource budget applies?
Which realized instruments have evidence relevant to that regime?
```

This does not require a scalar trust or capability score.

---

## 3. Empirical Seed: Repo Scout and Hermes

The first live Repo Scout pressure provides a bounded motivating example.

The Hermes realization returned a structurally complete packet with a valid terminal `ESCALATE`, but the retained result failed mechanical and semantic acceptance.

Observed failure included an invented execution basis and semantic collapse across several retained Turbo admission distinctions.

This evidence does **not** establish that Hermes is generally unreliable.

It does support separating at least two candidate transformation demands:

```text
exact coordinate conservation
!=
semantic variation / candidate generation
```

A realization that performs poorly when exact authoritative coordinates must survive may still be useful where controlled variation is desired and the consequence boundary prevents generated candidates from becoming authoritative state directly.

This projection therefore treats observed failure not only as a possible capability limit, but as evidence that realization selection may need to be relative to the intended transformation.

## 4. Candidate Transformation Regimes

The following are projections only. They are not established operator classes or routing categories.

### Conservation regime

Desired behavior:

```text
source distinction
        ↓
transformation
        ↓
recoverable source distinction
```

Candidate uses:

- exact coordinate copying
- provenance-sensitive extraction
- reconstruction
- bounded compression
- status preservation
- missingness preservation

Candidate requirement:

literal or relational identity must survive to the degree declared by the task.

### Divergence regime

Desired behavior:

```text
retained source state
        ↓
bounded variation
        ↓
multiple candidate states
```

Candidate uses:

- hypothesis generation
- alternative framings
- candidate structures
- creative synthesis
- mutation of proposals
- search over possibility space

Here, semantic drift may be useful rather than pathological if lineage, uncertainty, authority, and admission boundaries remain explicit.

### Adversarial regime

Desired behavior:

```text
candidate / contract / interpretation
        ↓
pressure for failure
        ↓
counterexample / rupture / falsifier candidate
```

Candidate uses:

- counterexample generation
- contract attacks
- ambiguity exposure
- adversarial prompts
- falsifier generation
- synthetic edge cases

The product is not authoritative truth. It is pressure material.

### Implementation regime

Desired behavior:

```text
bounded specification
        ↓
mechanical or symbolic construction
        ↓
candidate implementation artifact
```

Candidate uses:

- patch generation
- repair candidates
- transformations in isolated worktrees
- test construction

Candidate implementation remains distinct from acceptance or commit authority.

## 5. Aimable Flywheel

The broader candidate dynamic is that the development flywheel may become **aimable** by changing the transformation regime and consequence target rather than simply increasing capability.

```text
desired consequence
        ↓
select observation / action basis
        ↓
declare conservation or divergence requirement
        ↓
select candidate realization
        ↓
execute bounded transformation
        ↓
observe consequence
        ↓
retain success, failure, cost, and residue
        ↓
update future realization eligibility
        ↺
```

This would make realization evidence useful for more than qualification.

It could eventually shape which transformation instruments are admitted for which classes of consequence.

The flywheel is therefore not projected merely as:

```text
more work -> better machinery -> more work
```

but as:

```text
observed consequence
-> better knowledge of transformation requirements
-> better selection of realized instruments
-> more targeted consequence
-> better observation
```

No current apparatus implements this loop automatically.

## 6. Candidate Conservation Contract

A recurring need may eventually justify a first-class declaration similar to:

```text
source_basis
requested_transformation
required_invariants
allowed_variation
desired_variation
consequence_ceiling
resource_budget
comparison_basis
acceptance_boundary
```

Candidate operator name:

```text
declare_conservation_contract
```

This name is provisional.

Its purpose would be to make explicit what the transformation is responsible for preserving or changing before realization selection occurs.

A conservation contract would not itself select a realization or authorize action.

---

## 7. Candidate Realization Selection Operator

If recurrence later earns machinery, a second candidate operator may become useful:

```text
select_realization
```

Candidate inputs:

```text
conservation / divergence contract
retained realization evidence
current resource state
current consequence boundary
current task scope
```

Candidate output:

```text
eligible realization candidates
relevant supporting evidence
known failure boundaries
unresolved capability questions
```

Selection would remain distinct from qualification, authorization, execution, and semantic acceptance.

```text
eligible realization
!=
qualified generally
!=
authorized action
!=
accepted result
```

No implementation of this operator is currently justified.

## 8. Controlled Divergence Zones

If divergent realizations recur as useful tools, the Lab may eventually benefit from bounded **exploration zones** where variation is intentionally admitted.

Candidate pattern:

```text
retained evidence
        ↓
DIVERGENCE SESSION
        ↓
batched candidate variation
        ↓
provenance-bearing candidate set
        ↓
conservation / adversarial evaluation
        ↓
surviving candidates
        ↓
re-entry into consequential work
```

The key distinction is:

```text
generated candidate
!=
retained observation
!=
accepted claim
!=
authorized consequence
```

This permits semantic drift to be useful without allowing drift to silently become authoritative state.

An exploration zone should therefore be understood as a bounded variation habitat, not as permission for generated content to bypass admission.

## 9. Candidate Operator Ledger

Repeated work may justify retaining candidate operators before promoting them into persistent machinery.

A minimal projected record could contain:

```text
candidate_operator
transformation_performed
required_invariants
allowed_variation
desired_variation
observed_recurrence
evidence_instances
known_failure_boundaries
promotion_status
```

The purpose is not to create an ontology of possible operators in advance.

The purpose is to remember recurring consequential procedures long enough to determine whether recurrence actually earns extraction.

Candidate rule:

> record repeated operator-shaped work cheaply; mechanize only when recurrence makes the transformation contract stable enough to pressure.

---

## 10. Relationship to Authority

Realization selection must not imply authority expansion.

A realization may become strongly evidenced for a transformation regime while remaining low-authority.

```text
transformation suitability
!=
legitimacy outside that regime
!=
authority
!=
ownership
```

The system may learn that one realization is useful for exact conservation and another for divergent search without allowing either realization to self-issue a larger consequence surface.

This preserves the current projected constitutional direction:

> increased competence or successful propagation does not automatically increase authority.

---

## 11. What Would Count as Evidence

This projection becomes more interesting if repeated pressures show that:

1. different realizations systematically succeed or fail under different transformation requirements;
2. the same realization behaves differently as required invariance changes;
3. explicit conservation/divergence contracts improve routing or evaluation;
4. controlled divergence produces useful candidates without increasing semantic contamination of authoritative state;
5. Codex/frontier review burden falls when realization selection is conditioned on retained transformation evidence;
6. recurring transformation procedures justify stable operator extraction.

Evidence against this projection would include:

- no stable relationship between transformation regime and realization performance;
- routing overhead exceeding the benefit of differentiated selection;
- conservation contracts adding ceremony without improving interpretation or consequence;
- apparent specialization disappearing under replication;
- supposedly distinct regimes collapsing into one simpler mechanism.

---

## 12. Current Boundary

The Lab has not established:

- a general model-selection theory;
- a formal conservation-contract schema;
- a persistent operator registry;
- exploration-zone runtime machinery;
- automatic consequence-directed routing;
- a stable taxonomy of transformation regimes;
- any general claim about Hermes, Qwen, or other model families.

The current warranted move is only to retain the projection so later recurrence can either recruit, refine, split, or reject it.

---

## 13. Compact Form

The projected bridge can be compressed to:

> A realization is a candidate transformation instrument. Its suitability is relative to the consequence being sought, the observation or action basis admitted for that consequence, and the invariance or divergence regime the transformation is required to realize.

And the projected flywheel becomes:

```text
aim consequence
-> qualify basis
-> declare transformation requirement
-> select realization
-> act / observe
-> compare consequence
-> retain evidence
-> alter future selection
```

The projection remains non-authoritative until repository evidence repeatedly forces some part of it into method or runtime machinery.
