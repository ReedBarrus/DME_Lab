# Consequential Economic Coordination Network

**Status:** PROJECTION
**Scientific standing:** NONE
**Architecture authority:** NONE
**Implementation authority:** NONE
**Economic / trading authority:** NONE

This document preserves a candidate product and research horizon emerging from DME_Lab discussion.

Repository evidence outranks this projection.

Nothing here authorizes connection of DME_Lab to real financial accounts, brokerage execution, custody, payment systems, external markets, or autonomous economic action. Nothing here is a trading strategy, market claim, product commitment, or assertion that the projected system can be built safely or profitably.

The purpose of this note is narrower:

> Preserve the possibility that provenance-bearing consequence coordination may become a useful common medium for heterogeneous human and machine economic actors.

The hypothesis is not that one model, agent, institution, or DME instance should control an economy. The projected value is interoperability at the level of observed state, claims, authority, commitment, execution, and consequence while participants retain independent cognition, ownership, and governance.

---

## 1. Motivation

Contemporary economic software usually separates several layers:

```text
human objective
market / business observation
analysis
recommendation
approval
execution
settlement
accounting
feedback
```

Machine-mediated systems can compress these layers into one another. A model may turn an observation into a recommendation; a tool may turn a recommendation into an action; another system may later remember only the action and lose the evidence, uncertainty, or authority basis that preceded it.

For a consequential system, the following differences may matter:

```text
observation
!= interpretation
!= valuation
!= proposal
!= commitment
!= authorization
!= execution
!= delivery / fill
!= settlement
!= realized consequence
```

A projected DME-like economic medium would attempt to preserve enough relation across these transitions that downstream participants can determine what happened, why it was allowed to happen, what remains unresolved, and what must be reconsidered if an upstream basis later ruptures.

The product horizon is therefore not simply:

> AI that trades.

A stronger projection is:

> A shared consequence/provenance medium through which heterogeneous human and machine economic actors can coordinate observations, proposals, commitments, resources, execution, settlement, and repair without requiring common internal cognition or centralized control.

## 2. Candidate Coordination Primitive

A participant may eventually need to communicate statements of the following general form:

```text
I observed X
from source Y
under conditions Z.

I interpret A
within boundary B.

I propose consequence C
under authority basis D.

I can commit E
if condition F is satisfied.

Execution produced G.
Settlement produced H.
Observed consequence I changes what is currently admissible next.
```

The exact grammar is not specified here.

The projection is only that economic coordination becomes safer and more interoperable when the system can preserve distinctions between:

```text
source
claim
interpretation
uncertainty
proposal
commitment
authority
resource budget
execution
settlement
consequence
repair
```

Participants should not need to share the same model architecture, reasoning process, organization, or objective function. They need only enough shared structure for consequence-bearing relations to remain legible across handoff.

This suggests a candidate principle:

> Interoperability at the level of consequence may be more important than interoperability at the level of cognition.

This is a projection, not an established result.

## 4. Why Economics Is a Strong Pressure Surface

Economic systems make semantic and authority failures expensive.

A representational collapse such as:

```text
"candidate purchase appears attractive"
→ "approved purchase"
```

is no longer merely a summarization error if it can move money, consume compute, create contractual exposure, or bind another participant.

Economic coordination therefore provides a severe pressure surface for several DME concerns already appearing elsewhere in the Lab:

```text
observation != interpretation
proposal != commitment
available evidence != retained evidence
authority != capability
mechanical success != semantic success
requested action != accepted commitment
```

Later economic experiments could test whether these distinctions survive repeated handoff under incentives, uncertainty, scarce resources, and adversarial pressure.

## 5. Synthetic Economies Before Real Consequence

The safest projected development path is not to connect an immature system directly to real capital.

A candidate progression is:

```text
synthetic economy
→ bounded agent economy
→ simulated resource allocation
→ paper market / paper treasury
→ tightly budgeted real economic consequence
→ broader coordination only if independently earned
```

Synthetic environments could expose agents to:

```text
scarce compute
competing tasks
prices
transaction costs
stale observations
partial information
counterparty failure
leverage constraints
resource budgets
conflicting objectives
repair obligations
```

without placing real external assets at risk.

Such environments could pressure whether provenance, authority, commitment, consequence, and repair remain distinguishable when agents have incentives to act rather than merely summarize.

## 6. Resource Scheduling as a Proto-Economic Surface

DME_Lab may encounter primitive economic structure internally before any explicit financial application exists.

A local automation scheduler already faces candidate quantities such as:

```text
worker capacity
compute cost
latency
context capacity
memory pressure
energy / thermal cost
qualification state
failure probability
frontier-review cost
human-attention cost
```

Routing scarce work across heterogeneous realizations creates a resource-allocation problem even if no currency is present.

A future scheduler could therefore become a bounded experimental surface for economic coordination before external monetary settlement is introduced.

This does not establish that market mechanisms are the correct scheduler architecture. It only identifies resource scarcity and competing consequence budgets as a potential empirical bridge between local automation and later economic systems.

## 7. Candidate Network Effect

If multiple independent systems can exchange provenance-bearing consequence state, value may increase nonlinearly.

A simple projected interaction could look like:

```text
Actor A needs a resource.
Actor B can provide it.

A issues a bounded request.
B evaluates the request and its evidence.
A and B negotiate a commitment.
Authority and resource budgets are checked.
Work is executed.
Result is verified.
Settlement occurs.
Observed consequence updates both parties' retained state.
```

The important property is that neither participant must surrender internal autonomy merely to coordinate externally.

This could allow the same substrate to mediate relationships among:

```text
humans
local models
frontier models
businesses
services
machines
other DME-like systems
non-DME systems through adapters
```

The medium would be useful only if it remains traversable without silently centralizing authority.

## 8. Economic Agency and Human Control

A future economic agent may be able to research, propose, simulate, negotiate, schedule, purchase, sell, or allocate resources.

That capability does not imply that it should autonomously receive unrestricted economic authority.

A projected governed chain might resemble:

```text
human objective
↓
observation / research
↓
interpretation
↓
proposal
↓
risk and consequence evaluation
↓
authority / budget check
↓
commitment
↓
execution
↓
settlement
↓
observed consequence
↓
repair / amendment if required
```

No arrow should be assumed to follow automatically from the previous stage.

This projection is coupled to the candidate constitutional pressures preserved separately in `Proto_Meta_Constitution.md`, especially human agency conservation, authority non-self-escalation, rupture/repair conservation, economic non-capture, and recoverable dependency.

## 9. Adversarial Economic Pressure

Economic systems should be expected to contain participants that are:

```text
mistaken
stale
strategic
self-interested
incompatible
compromised
adversarial
```

A provenance-bearing record is not automatically a truthful record.

Future pressure may therefore need to distinguish:

```text
I know where this claim came from
!= I believe the claim
!= I am willing to depend on it
!= I authorize it to cause consequence
```

Possible later adversarial experiments include synthetic source poisoning, false corroboration, stale authority, commitment ambiguity, resource exhaustion, strategic withholding, replay, fork disagreement, and repair resistance.

No such experiment should require uncontrolled real-world economic access in order to pressure the relevant distinctions.

---

## 10. Economic Non-Capture

A system may become economically successful while undermining the agency of the people who depend on it.

The projected economic goal is therefore not merely profitability.

A stronger candidate pressure is:

> The system should create enough distributed value to sustain its continued operation without requiring hidden extraction, coercive dependency, irreversible lock-in, or authority capture.

This does not define a business model.

It preserves a question future product work must answer:

> Can the system sustain itself economically while increasing rather than absorbing the practical agency of its participants?

## 12. Candidate Questions for Later Pressure

If this horizon becomes operationally relevant, useful questions may include:

1. What minimum distinctions must survive an economic handoff for another actor to commit safely?
2. Can proposal, commitment, authorization, execution, and settlement remain mechanically and semantically separate under repeated automation?
3. How should resource budgets be represented without manufacturing a universal scalar value function?
4. Can independent agents coordinate economically without sharing internal cognition?
5. What forms of provenance remain useful against strategic or adversarial participants?
6. Can a ruptured claim identify the economic decisions and commitments that depended on it?
7. What fraction of economic consequence remains reversible after a basis is invalidated?
8. Can local resource allocation create a sufficiently rich synthetic economy to pressure these questions before real money is involved?
9. Does increased automation preserve or erode meaningful human economic agency?
10. What economic value can the substrate create without requiring authority capture?
