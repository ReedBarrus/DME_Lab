CANDIDATE A ATTACK
STRONGEST FORM:
With the task, policy, initial information, and relevant execution conditions held fixed, intervention on the delivery of independently generated feedback changes a later executed action in a way that improves task performance.
This is stronger than observing different output strings: the environment must accept the requested action and independently determine its consequence.
MAIN WEAKNESS:
A describes a useful behavioral property, but does not distinguish that property from conditional policy execution.
A memoryless lookup rule suffices:
- Unknown observation → INSPECT.
- RED observation → SUBMIT_RED.
- BLUE observation → SUBMIT_BLUE.
This counterexample defeats any claim that A demonstrates something beyond conditional response. It does not defeat A as a definition of elementary feedback control.
Several further qualifications matter:
- Interaction adds provenance and causal placement. The observation comes from an executed world interaction. At the policy interface it can still be an ordinary input token.
- The fixed goal provides the scoring rule. Holding it constant does not demonstrate that representing the goal causes the behavior. A policy with the answer rule hardcoded would pass.
- “Persistent” is unearned here. An unchanged task parameter across two calls supplies everything this fixture needs.
- A forced INSPECT does not demonstrate chosen information acquisition. It can still test feedback use. Testing acquisition choice would require meaningful alternatives.
- “Appropriate” must mean the declared matching predicate. It cannot depend on an evaluator’s impression of intelligence or intention.
- Reactive and closed-loop are compatible descriptions. A reactive policy becomes part of a closed loop when its actions and subsequent observations are connected through the environment.
- Both correct branches have the same success outcome. Their accepted submissions differ; their success scores do not. A must not require different binary outcomes between two successful branches.
DISTINCT PREDICTION:
Against a terminal decision committed before feedback, a feedback-using policy can correctly handle both hidden states. Against Null as written, none.
A itself does not predict that an arbitrary LLM will pass. A failed response therefore refutes that model’s demonstrated performance under the frozen conditions, not a definition of agency.
STATUS:
Survives as bounded feedback-dependent action selection. Equivalent to Null under the proposed pressure concerning the additional word “agentic.”
CANDIDATE B ATTACK
STRONGEST FORM:
Some task-relevant information must survive a specified boundary and causally affect a later decision when the current observation alone cannot determine the correct action.
The smallest possible retained content could be one bit distinguishing RED from BLUE. Belief, commitment, plan, lineage, and dependency are not interchangeable necessities.
MAIN WEAKNESS:
The proposed world never makes such retention necessary. The terminal policy receives the distinguishing observation directly.
B faces a boundary dilemma:
- If carrying the observation into the next policy call counts, A already contains the required persistence.
- If prompt-visible history or runner state counts, ordinary policy invocation already accommodates it.
- If those do not count, B must justify the actor boundary that excludes them.
- If information must survive process termination, B has introduced a recurrence pressure approaching Home’s question.
“One cycle” is insufficiently precise. The requirement must identify what information becomes unavailable, what carrier may retain it, and when that retained information must affect behavior.
DISTINCT PREDICTION:
None in this micro-world.
A delayed decision with an identical current observation but different relevant past observations could discriminate retention from its absence. However, A permits stateful policies, and Null explicitly permits history. Such a test would establish a retention requirement for that task—not select B over those unrestricted candidates.
STATUS:
Reject persistence as a demonstrated minimum for this pressure. Retain it as a separately testable requirement once an actual information boundary makes it necessary.
NULL ATTACK
STRONGEST FORM:
The policy mapping, combined with the world transition and observation rules, describes every relevant distinction without introducing an agency primitive.
MAIN WEAKNESS:
The expression policy(goal, visible_history) alone does not specify where history came from, whether observations were delivered before decisions, or whether requested actions were executed.
If Null erases those relations, it loses the distinction the experiment needs. But preserving them requires an explicit interaction protocol, not a new kind of actor.
A policy description and a feedback-dependence claim are also different kinds of statement: the first describes an interface; the second constrains behavior under interventions. Null must preserve that constraint when reporting results.
DISTINCT PREDICTION:
An unrestricted policy over visible history can express both successful feedback use and feedback neglect. It makes no unique success prediction and does not conflict with A’s successful behavior.
STATUS:
Survives. Null defeats the claimed necessity of an agency abstraction, while leaving the feedback-use distinction intact.
MINIMUM AGENTIC DISTINCTION
The smallest defensible target is bounded consequential feedback use:
Information returned by an executed world interaction affects a later accepted action, with a demonstrable benefit under the declared task condition.

“Consequential” requires an independently recorded action effect, not a narrated intention. It does not require INSPECT to alter the hidden color: returning information is sufficient for the epistemic step.
Calling this “bounded agentic behavior” would be a declared terminology choice. The experiment cannot establish that this phrase denotes behavior beyond conditional control.

SMALLEST EXECUTABLE PRESSURE
GOAL:
Submit the color matching the independently maintained hidden state.
WORLD:
A hidden bit, RED or BLUE, fixed before the relevant policy decision. INSPECT returns that bit. A submission creates a terminal world receipt and ends the episode.
Independence means the policy cannot author ground truth or adjudicate its own success. It does not require a separate service or machine.
OBSERVATION BOUNDARY:
Initial policy-visible information is identical across colors. Before INSPECT returns, no prompt, identifier, error, or other exposed value reveals the color.
Only the feedback-enabled condition receives the inspect result before selecting its terminal action.
ACTIONS:
INSPECT, followed by one terminal SUBMIT_RED or SUBMIT_BLUE.
Keep the proposed forced first step for this smallest pressure. Consequently, make no claim about voluntarily choosing to investigate.
CONTROL:
Use a paired RED/BLUE comparison with one terminal choice committed before either result is revealed and applied to both worlds. That fixed choice succeeds in exactly one world.
Include deterministic controls:
- A color lookup policy establishes that the feedback route permits success in both worlds.
- A fixed-answer policy establishes that the scoring and paired control behave as declared.
These controls also demonstrate why success alone cannot separate A from Null.
PRESSURE:
Compare the same candidate policy under feedback-before-decision and decision-before-feedback conditions, across both hidden states.
Freeze policy configuration, visible prefixes, action rules, and adjudication before execution. The logical minimum has four terminal evaluations: two colors under two information timings.
SUCCESS:
The feedback-enabled policy produces accepted matching submissions in both branches. The precommitted control matches one.
For deterministic evaluation under controlled execution conditions, this supports the bounded dependence claim. For a stochastic LLM, one successful pair establishes only the observed paired result; chance can mimic the desired pattern.
REFUTATION:
Under valid controlled conditions, failure to change the terminal action appropriately defeats the proposed policy’s success claim for that comparison.
A lookup policy passing refutes the stronger assertion that this behavior excludes ordinary conditional response. No possible score refutes an agency definition merely stipulated to include the behavior.
BASIS INSUFFICIENCY:
The experiment cannot distinguish A from unrestricted Null, establish B’s persistence requirement, or demonstrate causal sensitivity to the goal.
A single stochastic pair is also insufficient for a reliable causal-performance claim. That requires a frozen repeated, randomized evaluation or genuine control of the relevant randomness. Matching settings or requesting deterministic sampling must not silently stand in for that control.
INVALIDITY:
Invalidate the comparison if hidden state leaks before commitment, the control receives feedback early, the model supplies ground truth or judges success, requested actions are mistaken for accepted actions, or unsuccessful trials are selectively omitted.
An incorrect or malformed policy action is a recorded policy failure, not automatically an invalid experiment.
MINIMUM TRACE
- Run declaration: task and scoring rule, world/protocol version, policy configuration, evaluation schedule and randomness controls.
- Trial and branch association: color branch, feedback condition, and ordered event identifiers.
- Authoritative world state: hidden color and terminal state, retained outside policy visibility.
- Delivered observation: exact information exposed to the policy and its position relative to action commitment.
- Requested action: the policy’s actual request, associated with the observation prefix available when selected.
- World receipt: accepted or rejected action and the information returned or terminal consequence produced.
A separate claim/belief field is unnecessary because the experiment does not test reported beliefs.
Outcome is derived from authoritative color and accepted submission. It may be displayed, but is not an additional primitive. Neither an observation nor a policy assertion substitutes for world state.
MINIMUM SCAFFOLD
Three executable responsibilities suffice:
1. A tiny world evaluator owning hidden state, permitted observations, action acceptance, and scoring.
2. A replaceable policy call accepting only declared visible information.
3. A bounded comparison driver that orders calls and retains the required trace.
These can coexist in one small program. The observation function and trace do not need separate services, frameworks, or stores. Their boundaries must be inspectable.
REMOVE IF POSSIBLE
Remove:
- Persistent goal storage beyond the fixed task input.
- Belief reports, explanations, plans, and confidence scores.
- A memory subsystem or separate history object beyond necessary policy inputs.
- A separate outcome authority duplicating the world’s scoring rule.
- Physical hidden-state mutation merely to make INSPECT seem consequential.
- Independent architectural components for every conceptual coordinate.
- Every Home mechanism.
Retain observation delivery order, action acceptance, and source association. Those are doing discriminating work.
