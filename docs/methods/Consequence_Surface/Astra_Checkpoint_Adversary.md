CROSS-PRESSURE COMPRESSION
The smallest useful description has three roles:
1. The realized policy input and action interface.
2. The selected and accepted action.
3. The authoritative fixture evaluation of that action.
The policy input includes wording, ordering, disclosed content, and the constraints supplied during generation. These cannot be replaced by an abstract “same information” label: row-order and delexicalization results show that such substitution can lose behavioral distinctions.
The compressed empirical statement is:
In this coupled realization, some changes to predecision representations accompanied changes in accepted selection. Authoritative fixture relations could also change the consequence of an unchanged selected action. Supplying a relevant distinction did not reliably ensure its useful application.

This defeats two stronger formulations.
“Information must cross an observation boundary to affect behavior” is too broad. Hidden world changes can alter executed outcomes without changing selection—the late binding cells demonstrate exactly that. Restrict the claim to discrimination in policy selection through the declared input channel. Even then, exclusion of other channels is partly an apparatus condition, not a newly discovered universal law.
“Before commitment” is a coarse description of the tested boundary. Early information was supplied before inference; late information was withheld throughout inference and commitment. No intervention isolated delivery after inference but before acceptance. The evidence does not identify a special cognitive event at commitment.
Ordinary conditional selection plus fixture evaluation remains sufficient. No additional agentic primitive is forced.

The earlier story should be conserved as a sequence of narrowed explanations:
- Baseline: P selected RED throughout.
- P1: reversing decoder enum order did not remove that pattern. This weakened an enum-order-only explanation, not every presentation explanation.
- P2: P switched between RED and BLUE with the mapping declaration. A context-independent RED preference was already insufficient.
- Row-order pressure: the first declared target color summarized P behavior; F discrimination appeared only in BLUE-first presentations.
